# Table Rock Dam — Dimension 02: History & Timeline

**Subject:** Table Rock Dam, White River mile 528.8, ~8 mi SW of Branson, Missouri (Taney/Stone counties). Owner/operator: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE), Little Rock District (SWL). Power marketed by Southwestern Power Administration (SWPA).
**Purpose of this file:** a sourced chronology emphasizing *physical change over time* (what was built when, what it looked like, what changed) and *events that produced photographs*, to support a highly detailed 3-D model.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-05.

## 0. Method and confidence notes (read first)

- All research was done with WebSearch only (WebFetch/curl are blocked in this environment). Search results return URLs plus an AI summary of page content; facts below are taken from those summaries and result titles. **I could not open any page**, so per-page details (e.g., exact wording, photo captions) are as reported by the search summaries.
- The session's shared WebSearch budget was exhausted after my 35th query (200/200 across eight parallel agents), so several planned follow-ups (contractor names, dedication speakers, 1960s–1990s gate-opening years, fatalities) remain open — see §6.
- URLs are recorded **exactly as returned by search results**. Nothing is inferred; where I infer an existence of a page I say "(unverified)".
- Confidence tags used throughout:
  - **[S]** = stated in a retrieved search summary / result title, with URL.
  - **[S-approx]** = stated in a search summary, but the summary blended several results so attribution to a single URL is approximate; candidate URLs listed.
  - **[BK]** = background knowledge I hold that was *not* confirmed by a retrieved result in this session. Treat as leads to verify, not facts.
- Where sources disagree, both values are recorded and the item is listed in §6.

## 2. Narrative by phase

### 2.1 Pre-history and the name (1900s–1941)

**The name.** Every source agrees the dam and lake are named for a natural "Table Rock" — a flat, overhanging rock shelf that "stands high above the White River about 1 mile downstream from the dam site" (Missouri State Parks), "an overhanging rock formation one mile downstream" (Encyclopedia of Arkansas), located "at the small community of Table Rock, Missouri on Highway 165 about a mile and a half downstream" (Wikipedia/Table Rock, Missouri). Branson.com adds a twist: the name came from "the original site selected for the dam — Table Rock Mountain," which was abandoned as not stable enough while the name remained. The outcrop was "a well-known tourist landmark in the early 1900s." [S-approx] Sources: https://mostateparks.com/page/table-rock-state-park/park-history-table-rock ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/table-rock-dam-and-lake-6610/ ; https://www.branson.com/learn/free-things-to-do-in-branson/table-rock-dam/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock,_Missouri
*Modeling note:* the formation is a downstream landmark on the MO-165 corridor, distinct from the dam abutments; a related lead is the "Table Rock, Missouri" community page.

**Private-power era.** Dam building on the White River began in 1911 with St. Louis investors organized as the Ozark Power and Water Company; Congress had granted permission in 1910 for a hydroelectric dam near Forsyth. The Ozark Beach plant — Powersite Dam — was completed in 1913, the first hydroelectric dam in Missouri, creating Lake Taneycomo. Henry L. Doherty's Cities Service acquired the venture when the original backers ran into money trouble; his southwest-Missouri utilities were branded Empire District Electric, and the dam company merged into Empire in 1927. "Soon after the completion of Powersite Dam (1913)… Empire District Electric announced that they would build a larger dam more than twenty miles upstream at Table Rock." [S] Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powersite_Dam ; https://beautifulozarks.com/tag/powersite-dam/ ; https://beautifulozarks.com/tag/table-rock-dam/ ; https://sgcld.thelibrary.org/lochist/periodicals/wrv/v8/N8/s84g.htm ; https://beautifulozarks.com/early-view-of-white-river-dam-aka-powersite-dam/
*Not captured this session:* the 1920s–30s Federal Power Commission licensing history of Empire's Table Rock project and why it lapsed (Depression) — open question.

**Federal authorization and Dewey Short.** Dewey Jackson Short of Galena, the "Orator of the Ozarks," is "best known for supporting the construction of Table Rock Dam." He "achieved his goal when Congress passed the Flood Control Act of 1941, which authorized the funding and building of Table Rock Dam," but WWII and then the Korean War delayed construction until the Corps began building "eight miles southwest of Branson, Missouri, in 1954." [S] Source: https://historicmissourians.shsmo.org/dewey-short/ . The Encyclopedia of Arkansas adds that the Bull Shoals project also competed for funds and that Table Rock "plays an important role in water resource development for the White River basin, most of which lies in Arkansas," as part of a flood-control system for the Arkansas Delta and the Mississippi drainage. [S] Source: https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/table-rock-dam-and-lake-6610/ . The Dewey Short Papers (C4515) at the State Historical Society of Missouri are the primary archive: https://files.shsmo.org/manuscripts/columbia/C4515.pdf .
[BK, unverified this session]: Short served 1929–31 and 1935–57, lost his seat in 1956 (to Charles H. Brown), and was Assistant Secretary of the Army (Civil Works) 1957–61 — i.e., he held that post at the 1959 dedication. Sister projects: Norfork (completed 1944), Bull Shoals (completed 1951; dedicated with Norfork by President Truman July 2, 1952), Beaver (completed 1966), Greers Ferry (1962). Verify before use.

### 2.2 Construction (1952/1954–1959)

**Start date.** The standard account (Encyclopedia of Arkansas, Joplin Globe, Wikipedia) is that the Corps' Little Rock District "arrived in October 1954" and construction ran 1954–1958. However, one retrieved item quotes an October 11, 1952 Kansas City Times headline — "Start A Big Dam: Barbecue And Music At Launching of 76-Million-Dollar Reservoir" — and the Branson Tri-Lakes News 60th-anniversary piece says construction "didn't begin until 1952." The most likely reconciliation is a ceremonial groundbreaking/"launching" in October 1952 (with early access-road or site work), followed by the main dam contract and full mobilization in October 1954 — but this is inference; see §6. [S-approx] Sources: https://www.joplinglobe.com/news/local_news/bill-caldwell-table-rock-dam-a-dream-half-a-century-in-making/article_b95781ba-4fce-11ed-97cf-430ab9e86a01.html ; https://www.bransontrilakesnews.com/news_free/article_bb503ec6-922b-11e9-bafc-2b604507deb9.html ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/table-rock-dam-and-lake-6610/

**Sequence.** First concrete: May 24, 1955, "and the rest of the construction quickly followed." The 1954 start "required hundreds of workers, millions of tons of concrete, and careful planning." Dam completed August 1958; powerhouse and switchyard completed 1959; power on line May 1959 with two units; the switch was thrown at 2:45 p.m. on June 14, 1959; lake declared operational for public use in 1960. [S] Sources: https://www.bransontrilakesnews.com/news_free/article_bb503ec6-922b-11e9-bafc-2b604507deb9.html ; https://www.swl.usace.army.mil/Missions/Recreation/Lakes/Table-Rock-Lake/Dam-and-Lake-Information/ ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/table-rock-dam-and-lake-6610/

**As-built form (for the model).** Combination concrete-gravity and earthen-embankment dam: total length 6,423 ft; rises 252 ft above the riverbed; concrete section 1,602 ft with 1.23 million cu yd of concrete; a 531-ft spillway section with ten crest gates; embankment 4,821 ft with 3.32 million cu yd of fill; cost $65 million. [S] Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock_Lake . Powerhouse: four Francis turbine units of 50 MW each (200 MW) with Westinghouse generators. [S] Source: https://www.power-technology.com/marketdata/table-rock-us/

### 2.5 Dam safety and the auxiliary spillway (1990s–2005)

"Table Rock Dam's spillway capacity was evaluated as a result of a dam safety program in the 1990s. Using improved weather data and more modern technology and safety requirements, engineers determined that the lake would rise ten feet higher during the worst-case flood than previously calculated. An event of this magnitude would overtop the earthen embankment and destroy Table Rock Dam with catastrophic losses in downstream areas, including Branson. After considering several options and gathering considerable public input, an auxiliary spillway was determined to be the best solution." Congress approved construction "at a cost of approximately $65 million." [S-approx] Sources: https://www.betterbranson.com/item/table-rock-dam/ ; https://www.swl.usace.army.mil/Portals/50/docs/tablerocklake/Table%20Rock%20Dam%20Safety%20Fact%20Sheet%2020170313.pdf

**Phase 1 (Kiewit):** "excavating, blasting and preparing the foundation… 254,000 cubic yards of excavation." https://www.kiewit.com/projects/table-rock-auxillary-spillway-phase-1/ . Advanced Construction Techniques also lists Phase 1 work (specialty foundation/grouting contractor — role not confirmed): http://www.advancedconstructiontechniques.com/Table-Rock-Auxiliary-Spillway-Phase-1.asp [S]

**Phase 2 (Granite Construction):** "Granite Construction Company of Watsonville, California, was awarded a US$43.4M contract to construct an auxiliary spillway, with completion of the project expected in 2004 at a total cost of approximately US$60M. The scope of the work included the construction of a new eight-bay gated spillway, earthen dam embankments, and approach channel excavation, and a highway bridge was constructed to relocate Missouri Highway 165 over the completed channel." [S] https://www.waterpowermagazine.com/news/spillway-work-continues-at-table-rock-lake/ ; Granite project listing: https://www.thebluebook.com/iProView/1191135/granite-construction-inc/material-suppliers//construction-projects/table-rock-auxiliary-spillway-136610/ . Completed 2005 with eight more crest gates. [S] https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/table-rock-dam-and-lake-6610/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock_Lake . Photo thread: https://bcgforums.com/threads/table-rock-dam-auxiliary-spillway.32929/

*Modeling implications:* post-2005 the complex has (a) the original 10-gate spillway in the concrete section; (b) a separate 8-bay gated auxiliary spillway with approach channel cut through rock, its own small embankment tie-ins, and a multi-span MO-165 bridge across the channel; (c) the auxiliary spillway has never been used for a flood release as far as any retrieved source indicates (its crest is set at the top of the flood pool — [BK], verify with the geometry agent).

## 6. Conflicts & open questions

**Conflicts found**
1. **Start of construction: 1952 vs 1954.** Kansas City Times headline of Oct 11, 1952 ("Start A Big Dam… Launching of 76-Million-Dollar Reservoir") and Tri-Lakes News ("construction didn't begin until 1952") vs. EoA/Joplin Globe/Wikipedia ("Little Rock District arrived October 1954"; "constructed 1954–1958"). Likely a 1952 ceremonial launching then 1954 main mobilization — needs newspaper verification.
2. **Completion: August 1958 vs August 1957.** EoA/Wikipedia/USACE say 1958; one Branson source says "completed in August of 1957" (probably an error, or refers to the concrete section).
3. **Cost: $65M vs $66M vs $76M** (the last from the 1952 headline, i.e., the estimated project cost at launch).
4. **First power: "May 1959" (EoA, Wikipedia) vs "June 14, 1959, 2:45 p.m." (Tri-Lakes).** Plausibly first unit synchronized in May and ceremonial switch-throwing/dedication June 14.
5. **Auxiliary spillway cost: ≈$60M (International Water Power, 2003-era estimate) vs ≈$65M (Better Branson).**
6. **2017 peak release: 63,500 cfs (KY3, May 1) vs 68,000 cfs (Joplin Globe).** Also the 2011 figure of 68,000 cfs is variously called "record" — the 2015 72,000 cfs supersedes it.
7. **Kimberling City replacement bridge: 1956 (Better Branson) vs 1957 (rootsweb).**
8. **"Largest spillway release on record 12,500 cfs (1961)"** in Wikipedia/EoA is obsolete; 2008 (48,000), 2011 (68,000), 2015 (72,000) exceed it.
